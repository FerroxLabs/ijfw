# Book Phase Pattern

A book is a long-form narrative or expository artifact whose quality is judged less by speed and more by coherence across hundreds of pages. The workflow stretches all five phases over weeks or months, and the dominant risk is not bugs — it's losing the thread between sessions. Memory and outlines are load-bearing.

## Phase: Think

Establish the book's reason to exist before writing a sentence. Pin the premise (one sentence: "This book argues / tells / teaches X for Y reader"), the target reader, and the change the reader should feel by the last page. For fiction, also pin the protagonist's arc and the central conflict. For non-fiction, pin the thesis and the 3-7 supporting pillars.

Output is a `BOOK.md` that captures premise, audience, comp titles, tone, and a one-paragraph synopsis. Beat sheet (fiction) or pillar map (non-fiction) is drafted here.

Done signal:
- Premise sentence survives a "so what?" test from a trusted reader
- Synopsis fits on one page without hand-waving
- Beat sheet / pillar map has no orphan beats and no gaps in the arc

## Phase: Plan

Decompose the book into chapters with a target word count and a one-line purpose for each chapter. Per-chapter research list is captured here — every fact, anecdote, interview, citation needed. Sequencing matters: dependency-order chapters that build on prior reveals.

Done signal:
- Chapter list with word-count targets summing within 10% of book length goal
- Each chapter has a one-line "what this chapter delivers" purpose
- Research backlog enumerated per chapter (interviews booked, sources identified)
- Continuity ledger seeded (character ages, timeline anchors, recurring symbols)

## Phase: Build

Draft chapter-by-chapter, but verify-as-you-write — at the end of each chapter run a mini-verify pass (does it deliver the one-line purpose? does it contradict the continuity ledger? does it earn the next chapter's setup?). Don't wait for the whole manuscript to surface continuity errors.

Track word count and chapter status (drafted / revised / final) in a simple ledger. Memory is critical: cross-session, the last thing you wrote and the next thing you planned must survive a context flush.

Done signal:
- All chapters at "drafted" status
- Continuity ledger updated after each chapter
- No chapter ends with a hook the next chapter ignores

## Phase: Verify

Beta-read pass: 2-5 readers from the target audience read the full manuscript and flag confusion, dragging sections, and broken arcs. Continuity audit: sweep the ledger against the manuscript for age/timeline/name inconsistencies. Structural pass: does the macro arc deliver the promise from Think?

Done signal:
- Beta reader feedback collected and triaged into must-fix / nice-to-fix / ignore
- Zero continuity contradictions
- Macro arc audit confirms the Think-phase premise is delivered

## Phase: Ship

Final copy edit (line-level, not structural — that work belongs in Verify). Format for target distribution (print, ebook, audiobook). Publish through chosen channel (KDP, traditional publisher milestone, self-host). Launch announcement and first-90-day reader-feedback collection plan.

Done signal:
- Copy-edited manuscript signed off
- Formatted files validated on target devices/print proof
- Live on distribution channel(s)
- Launch comms scheduled and reader-feedback channel open

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Continuity is the silent killer.** A continuity ledger updated every chapter beats a heroic Verify-phase sweep.
- **Word count targets drift.** Re-plan when a chapter blows 50% over its target — it's a signal the chapter is two chapters.
- **Memory entries to watch:** "last chapter drafted," "next research blocker," "beta reader said X about chapter Y." These survive context flushes.
- **Skip-not-skim trap:** Verify phase tempts you to skim the middle. Don't. Middle-of-book sag is the most common structural failure.
- **Ship is not done.** First 90 days post-launch belong to Ship — reader feedback shapes the second printing or sequel pitch.
